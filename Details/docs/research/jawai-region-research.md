# Jawai Region & Ecosystem Research

## 1. Geographic Definition & Administrative Context
- **State:** Rajasthan, India
- **District:** Pali District (bordering Sirohi and Jalore districts)
- **Primary Hubs:** Sumerpur (sub-district headquarters), Sheoganj, Falna (nearest major railway junction)
- **Villages in the Wildlife Arc:** Bera, Sena, Perwa, Varawal, Bisalpur, Velar, Dudhiya, Kothar, Chamunderi
- **Coordinates:** 25.10° N, 73.17° E (Jawai Bandh Reservoir)
- **Topography:** Pre-Cambrian Aravalli granite plutons, flat sandy scrub plains, riverbeds, and expansive wetland margins.

## 2. The Granite Kopjes of Jawai
The distinctive visual signature of Jawai consists of dramatic, monolithic granite formations known geologically as *inselbergs* or *bornhardts*, weathered over 850 million years into smooth, rounded boulders and cavernous crevices.
- **Natural Den Sites:** Leopards utilize deep cave systems inside the granite hills for sheltering during daytime heat, rearing cubs away from hyenas and feral dogs, and observing the plains below.
- **Thermal Regulation:** Granite warms up during winter mornings, offering leopards sunning platforms with panoramic visibility, enabling high daytime sighting rates compared to dense jungle reserves like Ranthambhore or Jim Corbett.

## 3. Jawai Bandh (The Dam)
- **History:** Commissioned in 1946 by Maharaja Umaid Singh of Jodhpur and completed in 1957.
- **Scale:** Largest water reservoir in Western Rajasthan, spanning over 500 square kilometers at full capacity, fed by the Jawai River (a tributary of the Luni).
- **Ecological Function:** Wintering ground for tens of thousands of migratory waterfowl, resident mugger crocodiles (*Crocodylus palustris*), and seasonal breeding habitat.

## 4. Seasonal Dynamics & Climate
| Season | Months | Temperature Range | Wildlife & Landscape Character |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Peak Winter** | November – February | 8°C – 26°C | Prime wildlife viewing; leopards bask on granite rocks from 07:00–11:00; tens of thousands of migratory birds (flamingos, cranes, pelicans); brisk mornings. |
| **Pleasant Spring** | March – April | 18°C – 34°C | Clear skies, excellent morning light for photography, active leopard movements, decreasing water levels at the dam. |
| **Dry Summer** | May – June | 28°C – 43°C | Intense heat; leopards strictly active at dawn and dusk; waterhole concentration; maximum crocodile activity; dramatic sun-bleached desert landscapes. |
| **Monsoon Renewal** | July – September | 24°C – 35°C | Lush emerald vegetation blanketing the granite hills; waterfalls cascading off rocks; dramatic cloudscapes; challenging off-road tracks; highly dramatic photography. |
| **Autumn Transition** | October | 20°C – 33°C | Beginning of safari season; birds begin arriving; pleasant evenings; post-monsoon greenery fading to golden savannah. |
