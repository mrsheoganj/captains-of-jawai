# Rabari Culture & Human-Wildlife Coexistence Research

## 1. Who Are the Rabari?
The Rabari (also known as Raika or Dewasi) are an indigenous pastoral community inhabiting Rajasthan and Gujarat with a mythological origin tracing back to Lord Shiva. 
- **Traditional Livelihood:** Pastoral camel herding, sheep, and goat husbandry.
- **Iconic Attire:** 
  - **Men:** Distinctive voluminous scarlet or crimson turbans (*Pheta*), white pleated tunics (*Angrakhi*), silver neck torque necklaces (*Hasli*), gold hoop earrings (*Murki*), and carved wooden herding staves.
  - **Women:** Black embroidered ghagras, brass and silver anklets, and intricate hand tattoos (*Trava*).

## 2. The Philosophy of Coexistence
The core reason leopards survive in high densities alongside human settlements in Jawai without retaliatory killings (unlike in many other regions of India and Africa) is rooted in Rabari spiritual beliefs:
- **Devotion to Chamunda Devi & Ambika:** Leopards are considered divine protectors and the living vahan (steed) of the mother goddess.
- **Acceptance of Predation:** When a leopard occasionally takes a goat or sheep, the Rabari traditionally view it as a sacred tax or offering to the deity rather than an act of theft.
- **Zero Retaliatory Poisoning:** Unlike agricultural communities that trap or poison apex predators, the Rabari maintain an attitude of reverent acceptance, creating a rare sanctuary of peaceful coexistence.

## 3. Ethical Tourism Representation
Captains of Jawai treats the Rabari community not as human photo exhibits, but as equal custodians and spiritual protectors of the land:
- Guided village walks must be respectfully conducted with local community consent.
- Photography of elders, women, and homes must follow strict consent protocols.
- Tourism operations must directly contribute to village well-being, guide compensation, and local employment.
