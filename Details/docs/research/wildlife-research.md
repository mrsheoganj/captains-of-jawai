# Wildlife Research: Fauna of the Jawai Landscape

## 1. The Indian Leopard (*Panthera pardus fusca*)
- **Estimated Local Population:** 60 to 75+ free-roaming individuals documented across the 100+ granite hills of the Jawai-Bera-Sena landscape (exact scientific census numbers fluctuate; avoid claiming an exact official count without citing forest department census reports).
- **Unique Behavioral Adaptations:**
  - **Granite Cave Dwellers:** Rather than roosting in dense tree branches, Jawai leopards have adapted to cavernous boulder systems.
  - **Non-Aggression Towards Humans:** Over generations of peaceful coexistence with the Rabari pastoralists, these leopards display remarkably low hostility toward humans and vehicles, allowing calm, unhurried, naturalistic observations at ethical distances.
  - **Nocturnal & Diurnal Balance:** During winter months, leopards frequently remain on granite slopes well into late morning (09:00–11:00) basking in the sun.
  - **Dietary Composition:** Primarily feral cattle, wild boar, nilgai calves, langurs, peafowl, and occasionally domestic goats (compensated via community mechanisms).

## 2. Mugger Crocodile (*Crocodylus palustris*)
- **Habitat:** Jawai Bandh reservoir, feeding canals, and marshy river inlets.
- **Population:** Over 300 to 400+ adult crocodiles inhabit the reservoir.
- **Observational Opportunities:** During winter and dry summer months, groups of 10 to 30 crocodiles bask openly on sandy banks and granite shores alongside waterbirds.

## 3. Avian Diversity (Resident & Migratory Birds)
Jawai Bandh is a prime wintering wetland on the Central Asian Flyway:
- **Flagship Migrants:** Greater Flamingo (*Phoenicopterus roseus*), Lesser Flamingo (*Phoeniconaias minor*), Demoiselle Crane (*Grus virgo*), Bar-headed Goose (*Anser indicus*), Greylag Goose (*Anser anser*), Dalmatian Pelican, Great White Pelican.
- **Raptors:** Osprey, Crested Serpent Eagle, Tawny Eagle, Steppe Eagle, Peregrine Falcon, Short-toed Snake Eagle.
- **Resident Waterbirds:** Painted Stork, Woolly-necked Stork, Black-necked Stork, Eurasian Spoonbill, Indian Skimmer, River Tern.

## 4. Other Mammals and Reptiles
- **Striped Hyena (*Hyaena hyaena*):** Nocturnal scavengers inhabiting caves; frequently sighted on twilight safari drives.
- **Sloth Bear (*Melursus ursinus*):** Occasional sightings in dense boulder tracts and scrub thickets.
- **Indian Gray Wolf (*Canis lupus pallipes*) & Desert Fox (*Vulpes vulpes pusilla*):** Inhabit the open sandy scrub plains surrounding the granite hills.
- **Jungle Cat (*Felis chaus*) & Asiatic Wildcat (*Felis lybica ornata*).**
- **Blue Bull / Nilgai (*Boselaphus tragocamelus*), Chinkara (*Gazella bennettii*), and Indian Hare.**
- **Northern Plains Gray Langur (*Semnopithecus entellus*):** Major prey base and alarm caller for leopards.
