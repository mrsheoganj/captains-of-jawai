About Jawai
Leopards, granite hills and a working countryside
Jawai is a stretch of granite hills around the Jawai Dam in Pali district, roughly halfway between Udaipur and Jodhpur. Leopards den in the caves and rest on the rocks here, a short walk from villages, farms and hill temples. The Rabari herders who graze their animals on these hills have lived alongside them for generations.
It isn't a fenced national park. Our drives use village tracks and go out early in the morning and late in the afternoon, which is when the leopards are most often out on the rocks.

About Jawai
Seasons
Jawai Dam through the seasons
Best time to visit
Cool and clear
Winter · November to February
Cool, clear mornings, often 8 to 12°C at dawn. The reservoir is full after the rains and migratory birds arrive in thousands: bar-headed geese, demoiselle cranes and flamingos. Leopards bask on the warm rock. The best season for safaris.
Hot and dry
Summer · March to June
Hot and dry, often above 40°C by May. The water drops and sandbanks appear where mugger crocodiles bask, and wildlife gathers near what water is left. Drives run very early and late in the day.
Green and wet
Monsoon · July to September
Rain turns the granite hills green and refills the dam; in very wet years the reservoir overflows. Big skies, waterfalls on the rocks and few visitors. Some tracks get muddy, so drives can change at short notice.

Jawai culture
Herders and leopards on the same hills
The Rabari are pastoralists who have grazed sheep, goats, cattle and camels around Jawai for generations. Many hill shrines here are linked to local deities, and leopards are generally left alone, even when they take livestock. That tolerance is a big part of why the leopards are so relaxed around people. On our drives you pass their herds and hill shrines. We ask guests to keep a respectful distance and to ask before photographing anyone.

Village life
Life in Jawai's villages
Jawai culture
Jawai is lived-in country. Around Bera, Sena and the other villages, Rabari herders take their flocks out at dawn, women in bright odhnis carry water and fodder along the lanes, and bullock carts still share the tracks with safari jeeps. Ask your driver to stop at a village tea stall: a smile and a question go a long way. Please ask before you photograph anyone.
Questions
Before you book
All questions
When is the best time to visit?
October to March is prime season with cool mornings, basking leopards and migratory birds. April to June is hot but rewarding; the monsoon (July–September) is lush and dramatic.
How many drives should we book?
Three or four drives (morning and evening on two days) give a fair chance of good leopard sightings. Photographers often book more.
How do we reach Jawai?
Udaipur Airport is about 130 km (2.5 to 3 hours by road) and Jodhpur Airport about 175 km (3.5 hours). The nearest railway station, Sumerpur Jawai Bandh, is about 18 km away, and Falna about 37 km. We run safaris only and do not arrange transfers, but we are happy to suggest options.
Are leopard sightings guaranteed in Jawai?
No ethical operator can guarantee a wild animal sighting, and anyone who promises one is misleading you. However, because Jawai's leopards live on open granite rather than in dense forest, the probability of observing them over a 2–3 day stay is very high.
How is Jawai different from Ranthambhore or Jim Corbett?
Jawai is an open, lived-in landscape rather than a gated national park. There are no rigid zones or shared canters; drives are in private 4x4 vehicles with local trackers, and you can combine leopards with wetland birding and Rabari culture.
