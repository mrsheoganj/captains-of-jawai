A guide to the leopard hills Jawai, Rajasthan
Jawai is a stretch of granite hills, farmland and villages around the Jawai Dam in Pali district, Rajasthan. It is best known for leopards that live in the rocks, close to people.
Most people hear about Jawai because of its leopards. They live in the caves and cracks of the granite hills around the villages of Bera, Sena, Perwa and their neighbours, and on a good morning you can watch one sitting on a rock in the open sun. The rest of Jawai is just as interesting: a large reservoir built in the 1940s and 50s, winter birds on the water, crocodiles on the banks, and the Rabari herders who have shared these hills with leopards for a very long time.
Where is Jawai?
Jawai sits in the south of Pali district in western Rajasthan, roughly halfway between Udaipur and Jodhpur . The nearest town of any size is Sumerpur . The area takes its name from the Jawai River and the Jawai Dam (locally Jawai Bandh ; bandh means dam). On a map, look for the reservoir west of the Aravalli range, near the small town of Falna on the Ahmedabad railway line.
The Jawai Dam
The dam was built on the Jawai River, a tributary of the Luni. It was planned by Maharaja Umaid Singh of Jodhpur, work began in 1946 and it was finished in 1957. It is the largest dam in western Rajasthan and still supplies drinking water and irrigation to towns and farms across Pali and beyond. In a dry state, that water is what makes Jawai's wildlife possible. Read more about the Jawai Dam .
Leopards in the granite
The hills here are made of old granite, weathered into domes, boulder piles and caves. Leopards use the caves to rest and raise cubs, and the warm rock to bask on winter mornings. Local estimates put the population at around 50 to 70 leopards in the wider Jawai and Bera area; there is no official count for Jawai alone. There are no tigers or lions, so the leopard is the largest predator. About 20 square kilometres around the dam is protected as the Jawai Bandh Leopard Conservation Reserve , managed by the Rajasthan Forest Department. Jawai is not a national park and it is not fenced: villages, temples and fields sit right among the leopard hills. More on the leopards of Jawai .
People and leopards
The Rabari are a pastoral community known for their red turbans and herds of sheep, goats and camels. Many Rabari families here treat the leopard with tolerance and respect, linked to their faith and the hill shrines found on many rocks. Leopards do sometimes take livestock, and compensation is paid by the forest department. Conflict with people has been rare compared with many other parts of India, which is a large part of why Jawai's leopards are so relaxed in the open. About the Rabari .
Birds and other wildlife
The reservoir draws large numbers of birds in winter, including bar-headed geese, demoiselle cranes, flamingos, ruddy shelducks and painted storks. Mugger crocodiles bask on the sandbanks. On land you may see striped hyena, jungle cat, Indian fox, nilgai and grey langur, whose alarm calls often give away a leopard. Birds and wildlife of Jawai .
When to go
October to March is the main season, with cool mornings and leopards out on the rocks. December and January are the coldest months and best for winter birds. April to June is very hot, and July to September brings the monsoon, green hills and rough tracks. Month-by-month guide .
Getting there
Udaipur airport is the usual way in: about 130 km, or two and a half to three hours by road. Jodhpur airport is about 175 km to the north. By train, Sumerpur Jawai Bandh station is about 18 km from Bera and Falna about 37 km, both on the line between Ahmedabad and Jaipur. How to reach Jawai .

Questions people ask
Where is Jawai?
Jawai is in Pali district in western Rajasthan, India, roughly halfway between Udaipur and Jodhpur, near the towns of Sumerpur and Falna.
What is Jawai famous for?
Leopards that live among granite hills and caves close to villages, the Jawai Dam reservoir, winter birds and the Rabari herding community.
Is Jawai a national park?
No. Part of the area is protected as the Jawai Bandh Leopard Conservation Reserve, but there is no fence and no park gate. Villages, farms and temples sit among the leopard hills.
How many leopards are there in Jawai?
Local estimates usually put the number at around 50 to 70 leopards in the wider Jawai and Bera area. There is no official census figure for Jawai alone, and numbers change over time.
Is a leopard sighting guaranteed in Jawai?
No. Sightings are frequent because leopards rest on open rock, but they are wild animals and no honest operator can guarantee one.
What is the best time to visit Jawai?
October to March. Mornings are cool and leopards often bask on the rocks. December and January are best for migratory birds.

Seasons
Jawai Dam through the seasons
Best time to visit
Cool and clear
Winter · November to February
Cool, clear mornings, often 8 to 12°C at dawn. The reservoir is full after the rains and migratory birds arrive in thousands: bar-headed geese, demoiselle cranes and flamingos. Leopards bask on the warm rock. The best season for safaris.
Hot and dry
Summer · March to June
Hot and dry, often above 40°C by May. The water drops and sandbanks appear where mugger crocodiles bask, and wildlife gathers near what water is left. Drives run very early and late in the day.
Green and wet
Monsoon · July to September
Rain turns the granite hills green and refills the dam; in very wet years the reservoir overflows. Big skies, waterfalls on the rocks and few visitors. Some tracks get muddy, so drives can change at short notice.
