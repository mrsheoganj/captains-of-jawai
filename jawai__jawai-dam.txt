Jawai guide Jawai Dam (Jawai Bandh)
The largest dam in western Rajasthan, built under Maharaja Umaid Singh of Jodhpur, and the reason Jawai has water, birds and crocodiles.
The Jawai Dam, called Jawai Bandh locally, holds back the Jawai River near Sumerpur in Pali district. It is the largest dam in western Rajasthan and the reservoir behind it gives the whole area its name.
History
The dam was the idea of Maharaja Umaid Singh of the princely state of Jodhpur, after repeated droughts and floods in the region. Construction started in 1946 and the dam was completed in 1957, after Indian independence, by which time the project had passed to the new state of Rajasthan.
Jawai Dam in numbers
River Jawai, a tributary of the Luni
Location Near Sumerpur, Pali district, Rajasthan
Work started 12 May 1946
Completed 1957
Catchment area About 720 km²
Gross storage About 207.5 million m³ (active storage about 193.5 million m³)
Reservoir surface About 13 km² when full
Feeder dams Sei Dam and Kalibor Dam divert extra water into Jawai
Irrigation A command area of roughly 100,000 acres across Pali and Jalore districts
Figures are as published; some sources differ slightly.
What the dam does
The reservoir provides drinking water to Pali and nearby towns and villages, and irrigation water to farms downstream in Pali and Jalore. A hydro-power plant was part of the early plan but was dropped, because the water level could not give enough pressure all year. In years of poor rain the water level drops a long way, which shows how much the area depends on it. The Jawai River is a tributary of the Luni, the main river of western Rajasthan.
Wildlife on the reservoir
The shallows and sandbanks are home to mugger (marsh) crocodiles , which are easy to see basking on sunny winter days. From November to February the water fills with migratory birds: bar-headed geese, demoiselle cranes, greater flamingos, ruddy shelducks, pintails and many waders. Ospreys and other raptors hunt over the water. See birds and wildlife .
Visiting
The dam wall and the shoreline are easy to reach by road from Sumerpur and Bera. Sunset over the reservoir, with granite hills behind, is one of the best views in the area. Stay back from the water's edge: the crocodiles are wild. A wetland drive with a spotting scope is the best way to see the birds; ours is described on the wetland safari page.
Questions people ask
Who built the Jawai Dam?
It was planned by Maharaja Umaid Singh of Jodhpur. Work started on 12 May 1946 and it was completed in 1957.
How big is the Jawai Dam reservoir?
Published figures give a gross storage of about 207.5 million cubic metres, a catchment of about 720 km² and a reservoir surface of about 13 km² when full.
Which river is the Jawai Dam on?
The Jawai River, a tributary of the Luni, in Pali district, Rajasthan.
Are there crocodiles in Jawai Dam?
Yes. Mugger (marsh) crocodiles live in the reservoir and are often seen basking on the banks in winter.
Is Jawai Dam the largest dam in Rajasthan?
It is usually described as the largest dam in western Rajasthan.

Seasons
Jawai Dam through the seasons
Best time to visit
Cool and clear
Winter · November to February
Cool, clear mornings, often 8 to 12°C at dawn. The reservoir is full after the rains and migratory birds arrive in thousands: bar-headed geese, demoiselle cranes and flamingos. Leopards bask on the warm rock. The best season for safaris.
Hot and dry
Summer · March to June
Hot and dry, often above 40°C by May. The water drops and sandbanks appear where mugger crocodiles bask, and wildlife gathers near what water is left. Drives run very early and late in the day.
Green and wet
Monsoon · July to September
Rain turns the granite hills green and refills the dam; in very wet years the reservoir overflows. Big skies, waterfalls on the rocks and few visitors. Some tracks get muddy, so drives can change at short notice.
