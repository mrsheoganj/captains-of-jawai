Jawai guide The leopards of Jawai
Why leopards live so close to people in Jawai, how they use the granite caves, and what a sighting is really like.
In most of India a leopard is a shadow at the edge of the forest. In Jawai it is often a spotted shape on a bare rock, sometimes a few hundred metres from a village temple. That is what makes the place unusual.
Why the granite matters
The hills around Jawai are granite outcrops, worn into rounded domes and stacks of boulders. Between the boulders are caves and deep cracks that stay cool in summer and sheltered in winter. A female leopard can raise cubs in a cave like this with little disturbance. In the morning and late afternoon leopards come out onto ledges to warm up and to watch the land below, which is when they are easiest to see.
How many leopards?
Local estimates are usually in the range of 50 to 70 leopards for the wider Jawai and Bera area; there is no official census figure for Jawai alone. The Jawai Bandh Leopard Conservation Reserve covers about 19.8 km², but leopards move freely across the farmland and hills outside it.
Living next to people
There are no large forests here, and leopards hunt what they can find: wild prey such as hares, peafowl and langurs, as well as dogs and village livestock. Losses of goats or calves are compensated by the state. What surprises most visitors is how calm the relationship is. The Rabari, who graze herds across these hills, generally accept the leopard as part of the land. Many rocks carry small shrines, and people and leopards use the same paths at different hours.
What a leopard drive is like
Drives go out at first light and in the late afternoon, in open 4x4 vehicles on village tracks and dirt roads. Trackers watch the ridgelines, listen for langur and peafowl alarm calls and look for fresh pugmarks in sandy stream beds. When a leopard is found it is usually on a rock some distance away, so binoculars and a long lens help. Some days you watch one leopard for an hour. Some days you see none.
Watching them responsibly
Keep a good distance and never block a leopard's route back to its cave.
Switch the engine off while watching.
No baiting, calling, spotlights or drones.
Leave quietly if the animal looks uneasy or too many vehicles gather.
Our own field rules are on the conservation and ethics page.
Questions people ask
Why are there so many leopards in Jawai?
The granite hills are full of caves for shelter and cubs, there is water from the Jawai Dam, and local people have largely tolerated leopards for generations.
Do Jawai leopards attack people?
Attacks on people are very rare in Jawai. Leopards here mostly avoid people and take wild prey, dogs and sometimes livestock.
What time of day are leopards seen in Jawai?
Early morning and late afternoon, when leopards come out of the caves to bask on the rocks and before they start moving at night.
Can I see leopards in Jawai in summer?
Yes, but drives are kept to early morning and evening because of the heat, and leopards spend more time inside the caves.
