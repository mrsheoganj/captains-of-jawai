Best time to visit Jawai

Weather, leopards and birds through the year, so you can pick the right month for your trip.

Short answer: October to March. Longer answer below, because each part of the year has something going for it.

Month by month

MonthsWeatherWildlife
OctoberWarm days, cooler nights after the rainsHills still green, first migrant birds arrive, leopards active
November to FebruaryCold mornings (can drop below 10°C), pleasant daysPeak season. Leopards bask on the rocks; cranes, geese and flamingos on the reservoir
MarchWarming up, clear lightGood leopard viewing, migrants start to leave
April to JuneHot, often above 40°CDrives only at dawn and dusk; wildlife stays close to water
July to SeptemberMonsoon rain, humidGreen hills and dramatic skies; tracks can be rough and some days are washed out

For leopards

Winter mornings are hard to beat. After a cold night, leopards come out on the rock to sit in the sun, often in full view. March and October are also good, with fewer visitors than the Christmas and New Year weeks.

For birds

Come between late November and mid-February, when the migrant ducks, geese and cranes are on the reservoir.

For quiet

The hot months and the monsoon are much quieter. If you can handle the heat, early summer mornings can still be rewarding.

Season by season

Winter (November to February): peak season

Clear skies, pleasant days and cold mornings. Leopards bask on the rocks in the early sun and the dam is full of migrant birds. It is also the busiest time, especially from Diwali to New Year. More: Jawai in winter.

Summer (April to June): hot and quiet

Afternoons often pass 40°C, so drives keep to dawn and dusk. Vegetation is thin, roads are quiet and wildlife stays close to water. More: Jawai in summer.

Monsoon (July to September): green and dramatic

The dam fills, the granite hills turn green and visitors are few. Rain can change routes or shorten a drive, and thicker cover makes leopards harder to spot. More: Jawai in the monsoon.

Safari timings through the year

Safaris run in the morning (about 6:00 to 9:00 AM) and evening (about 4:00 to 7:00 PM). Start times move a little with sunrise and sunset: earlier in summer, a touch later on winter mornings. See morning vs evening safari.

Planning tips

  • Book early for December, January and holiday weekends: one to two weeks ahead.
  • Stay two nights for three drives and a much better chance of leopards. See the 2-day itinerary.
  • Combine Jawai with Udaipur (about 2.5 hours), Jodhpur, Ranakpur or Kumbhalgarh. See how to reach Jawai.

What to bring

  • Winter: a warm jacket, cap or beanie and gloves for early drives in the open jeep
  • Summer: light, breathable, neutral clothing, a wide-brim hat and plenty of water
  • Monsoon: a rain jacket or poncho and a cover for your camera
  • All year: binoculars, sunscreen, sunglasses and a camera with a long lens

Questions people ask

Which month is best for Jawai?

December and January are the most popular, with cold mornings and the best chance of leopards basking in the open. October to March is the main season.

Is Jawai open in the monsoon?

Yes, Jawai is not a closed park, but rain can disrupt drives and tracks can be rough from July to September.

How hot does Jawai get in summer?

April to June regularly passes 40°C in the afternoon. Drives are kept to early morning and evening.

How many days do I need in Jawai?

Two nights is the minimum for a fair chance at leopards. Three nights gives you more drives and time for the dam and villages.

What is the best time of day for a Jawai safari?

Both drives are good: mornings (about 6:00 to 9:00 AM) are cool with soft light, and evenings (about 4:00 to 7:00 PM) bring leopards onto the warm rocks in golden light.

When should I book a Jawai safari?

For December, January and holiday weekends, book one to two weeks ahead. Outside peak season a few days is usually enough.

Seasons

Jawai Dam through the seasons

Best time to visit
Cool and clear

Winter · November to February

Cool, clear mornings, often 8 to 12°C at dawn. The reservoir is full after the rains and migratory birds arrive in thousands: bar-headed geese, demoiselle cranes and flamingos. Leopards bask on the warm rock. The best season for safaris.

Hot and dry

Summer · March to June

Hot and dry, often above 40°C by May. The water drops and sandbanks appear where mugger crocodiles bask, and wildlife gathers near what water is left. Drives run very early and late in the day.

Green and wet

Monsoon · July to September

Rain turns the granite hills green and refills the dam; in very wet years the reservoir overflows. Big skies, waterfalls on the rocks and few visitors. Some tracks get muddy, so drives can change at short notice.
