Places to visit in and around Jawai

Between leopard drives: the reservoir, the villages and hill shrines, and the forts and temples within a few hours.

Most visitors plan their days around two drives, at dawn and late afternoon. The middle of the day is free, and Jawai has plenty to fill it.

In Jawai
Jawai Dam

The reservoir is the centre of the area. Go in the late afternoon for crocodiles on the banks and the sunset over the water. In winter it is full of birds. About the dam.

Bera and the village hills

Bera is the village most associated with leopard sightings, surrounded by granite hills. Walking through the lanes, past fields and small temples, shows you how closely people and leopards live here.

Temples of Jawai

Many hills here have a shrine, from a painted stone under an overhang to a whitewashed temple at the top of a long flight of steps. Three are especially well known.

Dev Giri Temple (Ashapura Mata cave temple)

A cave shrine about halfway up a granite hill near Perwa and Bera, reached by a winding whitewashed staircase. It is dedicated to Ashapura Mata, the protector goddess of many local families. Leopards are regularly seen on the rocks and steps around the temple, especially around dawn and dusk, which has made it a famous symbol of how people and leopards share these hills.

Kambeshwar Mahadev Temple

An old Shiva temple in the Aravalli foothills near Sheoganj, roughly 11 km from Jawai Dam. It sits in a green valley with natural springs, granite boulders, langurs and peafowl: a peaceful stop between a morning and an evening drive.

Khodiyar Mata and Tankeshwar Mahadev temples

Two shrines on the rocky high ground by the Jawai Dam, with wide views over the reservoir, the sandbanks where crocodiles bask, and the granite domes beyond. Come at sunrise or late afternoon for the light.

All three are working places of worship: remove your shoes, dress modestly, keep your voice down and ask before taking photographs.

Rabari settlements

A visit with a Rabari family, arranged properly and with permission, is a chance to learn about herding life, traditional dress and how the community sees the leopard. Rabari heritage walk.

Sundowners on the rocks

Climbing a granite outcrop for the last light of the day is a Jawai tradition. Boulder sundowners.

Day trips
Ranakpur Jain temple

A 15th-century marble temple famous for its hundreds of carved pillars, about 50 km (around 1.5 hours) away. Combine it with Kumbhalgarh, about 80 km from Jawai.

Kumbhalgarh Fort

A hill fort with one of the longest continuous walls in the world, and part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for the hill forts of Rajasthan.

Mount Abu

Rajasthan's only hill station, known for the Dilwara temples and Nakki Lake, about 100 km (2 to 2.5 hours) to the south.

Udaipur and Jodhpur

Udaipur is about 2.5 hours away and Jodhpur about 3 to 3.5 hours, so both make natural start and end points for a trip to Jawai. See how to reach Jawai.
